# Delegated research and review reports

Updated 26 September 2026. This file records useful research conclusions for Supermarket: The Night. Reference projects were inspected for reusable design ideas; their gameplay code, art, audio, and distinctive presentation were not copied into this project.

## Codex worker — reference and content research

The worker cloned and inspected several top-down survivor projects and summarized the useful implementation patterns in [production_content_catalog.md](production_content_catalog.md):

- BESTAGON’s `01-survivor` (MIT): time-phased enemy schedules, weighted rosters, spawn cadence, and a living-enemy cap.
- `vampire-survivors-clone` by migalvalm (MIT): separation of clock, enemy spawning, XP collection, and upgrade selection.
- Slime Survivors (MIT code; separate GDQuest assets are CC BY-NC-SA): finite wave budgets and readable transitions. The separately licensed non-commercial art was not reused.
- Riftbound Survivors (GPL-3.0): explicit combat, cleanup, intermission, and result states. Its code and assets were not copied.

Asset candidates were also checked and recorded in the content catalog. The gameplay art selected here is original project art generated for Supermarket: The Night; existing CC0 Kenney files and original license records remain separately documented in [ATTRIBUTION.md](../ATTRIBUTION.md).

## Antigravity — independent second opinion

The Antigravity operator ran Gemini 3.8 Flash medium in `accept-edits` mode for a read-only mechanics and later integration review. The operator only launched and steered Antigravity; it made no separate implementation changes. The second opinion independently identified modular target selection, enemy spawning, XP drops, and upgrade choices as useful patterns, and agreed that co-op and shops were outside the current single-shift design.

The later static integration review found four concrete issues, all fixed in the project:

1. The HUD card margin block had incorrect indentation. It is now inside the card construction loop.
2. The enemy was added before configuration, so its `_ready()` saw a null definition. Arena now configures the actor before adding it.
3. A level-up number key could dismiss a choice when fewer cards were available. The HUD now limits numeric selection to the actual card count, and Arena rejects invalid or unapplied choices without resuming.
4. The weapon controller lacked `has_weapon()`, so duplicate unlock cards were not filtered. The query now checks the active per-run weapon state.

AGY’s review was static only; it did not edit project files, import resources, launch Godot, or run tests. Its results are separate from the Codex runtime checks below.

Codex’s subsequent runtime check found two more issues outside AGY’s reported findings: a `match` block indentation parse error in `enemy_actor.gd`, and a boss-bar variable name typo in `hud.gd`. Both were corrected before the final smoke runs.

## Codex implementation and runtime checks

The Codex worker implemented the player progression/modifier API, weapon catalog/runtime modes, and projectile behaviors in:

- `entities/player/player.gd`
- `combat/auto_weapon.gd`
- `combat/projectile/projectile.gd`

It preserved the legacy projectile and projectile-layer methods, added per-run weapon upgrades and level-up events, and supports targeted, orbiting, returning, and deployed slow-zone attacks. It also added health restoration and expiring movement slows/boosts.

The lead connected those APIs to the timed arena schedule and original store/actor/weapon/pickup art, then added the title, pause, upgrade, boss-health, and result UI. The theme and seven short sound cues are original synthesized WAV files generated by `assets/audio/generate_audio.py`; volume settings persist locally.

Codex separately used Godot 4.7.2 to import the project and start the title and arena scenes headlessly. The initial launches exposed a title theme-call error, an enemy-script indentation error, and the boss-bar variable typo; these were corrected. The final title startup and 900-frame direct arena launch completed without script/runtime errors, and `git diff --check` reported no whitespace errors. Headless mode deliberately skips audio device initialization, so the generated music/SFX and volume dialog still need a desktop audio-device check. These are smoke checks, not gameplay balance or full visual QA.

## Brotato wiki catalog count research — Codex worker

The reused Codex worker counted the community wiki tables and separated current table coverage from later official patch additions. The wiki lists 65 combat enemy types (66 named entries when neutral Tree is included): Crash Zone has 22 regular rows, 8 elites and 2 bosses; Abyss DLC has 23 regular rows, 9 elites and 2 bosses. Its weapon tables contain 47 melee plus 31 ranged names (78 identities; weapon tiers are variants). The wiki marks its weapon page Patch 1.1.6.3 and is stale against Steam patch 1.1.15, which adds 16 named enemy forms and Rail Gun. Adjusted comparison counts are about 81 combat forms and 79 distinct weapon names. Unnamed Endless Nightmare buffs are not extra identities. These are context, not Bakkal roster requirements. Sources and counting rules are in [gameplay_reference_notes.md](gameplay_reference_notes.md).

## Modular room art and scene plan — review in progress

The lead generated separate base-room concepts under `assets/generated/rooms/`: a larger market, a separate broad depot, a manager's office and a four-stall restroom. The user's correction requires the market's rear-left staff door to lead to the depot, with the office and WC only reachable from the depot. Each room also needs five consistent event states (clean start, entrance/connector open, lights out, messy mid-run, tidy lights on), with fixed door anchors and furniture geometry.

The reused Codex worker reviewed the room transitions read-only. Approximate door centers in normalized image coordinates: market staff door (0.129, 0.113); depot market exit (0.146, 0.867), WC exit (0.225, 0.100), office exit (0.767, 0.100); office depot entrance (0.718, 0.918); WC depot entrance (0.238, 0.919). Its recommendation is to use explicit door-portal scene transitions with fade and safe arrival offsets, not stack full-frame images into an overlapping continuous map. These estimates require exact placement during scene implementation.

The reused Antigravity operator ran a Gemini 3.8 Flash medium read-only art review in session 74655, accept-edits mode. It confirmed the rooms are separate, the market rear-left staff door is visible, the restroom has four stalls, and the overall palette is consistent. It flagged room tile scale in the office/restroom as about 1.5× larger than the market/depot, and called the depot's center rack framing heavier. The lead regenerated the office and WC base images to align tile density and adjusted the depot's central shelf framing. The user clarified that the main “thick border” complaint was oversized in-game collision, not the painted shelf edges; collider footprints are therefore being reduced to the rendered fixture base. The room-count/task review and source image list remain read-only. No agent changed project files or ran tests.

The reused Codex worker inspected local clone code and the live Godot definitions, then authored two enemy profiles, three weapon definitions, four passive upgrade resources and three weapon-unlock cards. The lead then loaded the new weapons/upgrades into the live offer catalogs and included both enemies only in late phases of the legacy five-minute schedule. The Antigravity operator used the existing Gemini 3.8 Flash medium session read-only. Those require additional runtime systems and remain design alternatives, not implementation decisions. Event geometry, hazards, defense objectives and their art are not yet in the game.

The helper and supply pickup have generated project-local art. Room access scenes and transition wiring are not in place yet, so these new room events are authored/runtime building blocks and are not yet playable through the main shift. The room-event production rule is recorded in [production_content_catalog.md](production_content_catalog.md): define spawn/interaction conditions, per-wave limits, rewards, expiry/failure and UI feedback before completing event art.
